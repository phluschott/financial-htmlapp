Know what each paycheck has to pay — and what’s left.

Paycheck Planner is a simple visual aid: it lays out where your money is going and when. It splits your month by paycheck and, for each one, lists everything that comes out before the next paycheck, in date order.

1 · It is only a visual aid. It shows you where your money is going and when. That is all it is designed to do. It does not tell you what to do with your money.
2 · It is not financial advice. Nothing in this app or on this website is financial, tax, legal, or investment advice. The numbers are only the figures you type in.

Built for the “what does the next two weeks look like?” problem

When bills pull money on different days and you are paid every other week, it is hard to hold it all in your head. This app does it for you.

By paycheck

Each paycheck is a card. Bills, subscriptions, credit card minimums, and transfers land on the paycheck that comes before their due date.

In date order

See every payment for that paycheck in the order it comes out, with a running “left after” figure — so you can see the day it gets tight.

What’s left to play with

Income minus what is due. Send the leftover to savings, a sinking fund, or an extra debt payment with one click.

Whole-month view

A month with two paychecks is two cards. Your next paycheck is highlighted, and landed income is kept separate from expected income.

Sinking funds

Named buckets (new car, house…) you add to when you choose and pull back from when you need to.

Spreadsheet export

Export any month, plus your bills and funds, to an .xlsx file.

Private by design

Runs on your computer

You download it and open it in your browser. It never needs the internet, and this website never sees your data.

No network access

The page ships with a Content-Security-Policy that blocks every network request, and the code is plain, readable JavaScript.

Optional encryption

Set a password and your data file is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using your browser’s built-in crypto. There is no recovery if you forget it.

Not a bank dashboard. It does not connect to your accounts and does not track balances. You tell it your income schedule and your bills; it lays them out so you can see them.

Get started

  1. Download the zip and verify the hash (optional but recommended).
  2. Unzip it into a folder you will keep, such as Documents.
  3. Open index.html in your browser (double-click it) and follow the short setup.
  4. In Chrome or Edge, choose that same folder when asked, and your data autosaves there with backups. In other browsers, use Save file and Open a data file.

Browser support

Chrome, Edge, OperaAutosaves finance-data.json into the folder you choose, and keeps timestamped backups.
Firefox, Safari, othersWorks fully, but cannot write to a folder. You download your data file with Save file and load it with Open a data file.

Good to know

  • Back up your data file. It is the only copy of your information. Keep it somewhere safe and private.
  • No password recovery. If you turn on encryption and forget the password, the data cannot be opened.
  • It is a visual aid, not financial advice. It does not recommend, evaluate, or approve any financial decision, and it comes with no warranty. If you need advice, talk to a qualified professional.
  • It is only as accurate as what you enter. Check amounts and due dates against your own statements.
  • Changing an amount (like a price increase) applies going forward; past months keep their old values.
